      Day 01Friday, 31 July

      Into the green hills

      San José → Zarcero

      We land, find the car, and point it north. The road climbs out of the valley into dairy country and rain, and ends in a cool little town famous for hedges clipped into archways and animals. Zarcero sits high in Costa Rica's central highlands: a gentle first look at the green, working landscape that feeds the country as surely as it photographs beautifully.

      Day 02Saturday, 1 August

      From clouds to steam

      Zarcero → Arenal

      A long switchback descent into humid rainforest, with the volcano showing up in the windscreen somewhere around La Fortuna. The afternoon is spent doing nothing at all, in hot water, under the canopy. Arenal's perfect cone is young in local memory: its sudden 1968 eruption reshaped the area. Today the national park protects the volcano's slopes and the water systems that feed Lake Arenal and nearby communities.

      Day 03Sunday, 2 August

      Above the canopy

      Místico hanging bridges

      Morning on the hanging bridges, level with the treetops and looking straight down into the green. We come back after dark for the night hike, when the forest swaps one whole cast of animals for another. This is the Arenal landscape at a smaller scale: rain-fed forest, volcanic ground, and a canopy that makes even a short walk feel like a chance to look into a different layer of the rainforest.

      Day 04Monday, 3 August

      Chasing water

      Waterfall rappelling & cacao

      The loudest day of the trip. Harnesses on in the morning and over the lip of a waterfall, one rope at a time. Dry clothes at lunch, then something much calmer: cacao pods, a stone grinder, and chocolate we make ourselves. In Arenal, the same wet tropical climate that keeps the streams full also makes this a natural place to follow cacao from its bright pods to the cup.

      Day 05Tuesday, 4 August

      A day with room to roam

      Arenal / La Fortuna

      Nothing booked. The plan is whatever the weather allows: the waterfall, a trail, a viewpoint, or one more long soak. Halfway through a trip, an empty day is the best thing on the itinerary. It is also time to notice how much the volcano sets the rhythm here: clouds collect around its summit, rain feeds the forest, and geothermal water emerges warm enough for the springs.

      Day 06Wednesday, 5 August

      Crossing to the Pacific

      Arenal → Uvita

      The big drive: nearly six hours south and west, from volcano country down to the coast road, and a final climb up a ridge above Uvita — where the pool stops and the whole Pacific starts. Uvita sits beside Marino Ballena National Park, whose tide-shaped sandbar is known as the Whale Tail. August also falls within one of the region's humpback-whale seasons, so the ocean may have its own surprises before we ever board a boat.

      Day 07Thursday, 6 August

      The wildest green

      Uvita → Corcovado

      A boat down the coast to San Pedrillo ranger station, then on foot into Corcovado — the densest, loudest, most alive place on the whole trip. Hot, muddy, and worth every step. Created in 1975 on the Osa Peninsula, the park protects a remarkable sweep of rainforest, beaches, mangroves, rivers, and marine edge. San Pedrillo is one of its gateways: a place to slow down and look for the everyday drama of monkeys, birds, tracks, and enormous trees.

      Day 08Friday, 7 August

      Blue, below the surface

      Uvita → Caño Island

      Back on the water, this time straight out to Isla del Caño. Masks on over the reef, and a long ride home watching for anything that breaks the surface. A day made entirely of blue. The island is a biological reserve, about 16 kilometres off the Osa Peninsula, with some of the Pacific coast's best-preserved coral formations. Its story reaches farther back, too: the island holds pre-Columbian stone spheres and other traces of Indigenous life.

      Day 09Saturday, 8 August

      Back over the mountains

      Uvita → Irazú → San José

      An eight o'clock start and one last long drive: off the coast, up through San Isidro and over the pass. We roll straight through Cartago and keep climbing — all the way up to the crater rim of Irazú — before dropping down to the airport and home. At 3,432 metres, Irazú is Costa Rica's highest active volcano; its 1963–65 eruption sent ash as far as San José. It is a fitting final view: a stark crater above the clouds after eight days spent inside the country's greenest places.
